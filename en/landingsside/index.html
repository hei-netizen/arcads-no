What is a landing page?

A landing page is the page people arrive on when they click an ad. It can be your homepage, but that is rarely a good idea. The homepage has to speak to everyone: job seekers, people looking for opening hours, existing customers. Someone who clicked on “move-out cleaning Oslo fixed price” wants to know one thing: what it costs and how to book.

In Norwegian it is called a landingsside. Many businesses use the word for any subpage, but we use it strictly: a page made for one ad group, one offer and one action.

The maths

Cost per click divided by conversion rate.

That is the whole reason we care about the landing page. You have little control over the cost per click. You have a lot of control over the share who get in touch.

Cost per lead = cost per click ÷ conversion rate. The conversion rate is the share of clicks that end in a form, a call or a purchase.

Take the moving company we run ads for in Oslo. A search click cost NOK 52 (June–Sept 2026). The same click price gives very different lead prices depending on how well the page does its job:

How we build a landing page

The structure below is where we start for service businesses that live on inquiries. Every point has a reason.

  1. One offer per page. One service, one area, one price or price level. If you have three services, we make three pages. Someone searching for roof cleaning should not have to scroll past facade cleaning and ventilation cleaning to find it.
  2. An H1 that answers the ad. If the ad promises fixed-price move-out cleaning in Oslo, the headline says so. When ad, keyword and page say the same thing, visitors see at once that they are in the right place. Google uses the same match when it sets Quality Score.
  3. The form high up on mobile. At Utvendig Renhold, 4,258 of 5,490 search clicks came from mobile (July–Sept 2026). The page has to work on a phone screen before it works on a big screen, and the form, or the button to it, should be visible without much scrolling.
  4. A one-step form with only the fields you need. Name, phone and what you need to give a price. Every extra field costs inquiries. Which fields to use is covered in lead generation for service businesses.
  5. Real photos. Of the people, the vans and the jobs you actually do. A page that looks made up won’t get anyone to hand over their phone number.
  6. Reviews at the top. The stars and number of Google reviews right under the logo, and some of the reviews further down. That is the first thing a new customer wonders: were others happy?
  7. An FAQ that answers the objections. Price, what is included, how soon you can come, what happens if something goes wrong. The questions should be the ones customers actually ask on the phone.
  8. No menu leakage. No main menu, no links to the blog or “about us” halfway down. Every outbound link is a way away from the form, and you have already paid for the click.

Online stores follow a slightly different recipe. There the goal is a purchase, and the page should get people to the products as fast as possible: a clear offer, shipping and delivery time at the top, and one button onward. QualiPet’s campaign page below is built that way.

Business leasing

16.1% on a new page

For a company that leases equipment to businesses, we made a new landing page in June 2026. In the first 30 days (to 13 Aug 2026), 16.1% of visitors from the ads got in touch, and a lead cost NOK 412. The page has one offer, and the headline answers what people searched for.

Business leasing

The page belongs on your domain

The page first sat on a subdomain of ours. It converted well, but Google rated the landing page experience “below average”, which drags Quality Score down. On 6 September 2026 we moved it to the client’s own domain. The lesson: ad traffic should land on the business’s own domain.

Utvendig Renhold

18 local pages, one per branch

Utvendig Renhold advertises in nine regions, with one campaign per region. Each branch now has its own local landing page, 18 pages in total. They went live in the ads on 28 Aug 2026, when 102 ads were switched to point to them. See the full case.

Tracking

The form has to count in Google and Meta

A landing page without tracking is a guess. When the form is submitted, a conversion is recorded in Google Ads, the Meta pixel and GA4. Forms, calls and bookings become separate conversions, so you can see what comes from where. At Leid.no, the tracking counted far more purchases than actually happened, before we fixed it.

What does a landing page cost?

We don’t charge separately for landing pages. We make them as part of the ad partnership, together with the ads, because page and ad have to fit together for the numbers to be good. The same goes for the ads we produce: everything is made in-house by the two of us.

For the partnership itself, we take a share of what we earn you, not a fixed monthly fee. If you don’t earn more, you pay us nothing. For it to work for both sides, the ads need to generate enough for our share to be around NOK 20–30,000 a month. That is why we work with established businesses with at least NOK 30,000 in monthly ad budget. More about pricing.

What we don’t do: we don’t build whole websites, and we don’t do SEO. Our landing pages are made for traffic from Google Ads and Meta, and they are measured by what a lead costs.

Questions about landing pages.

What is a landing page?+
A page made for one ad and one offer, with one goal: that the person who clicked gets in touch or buys. It has no menu to wander off into, and it is measured by the share of visitors who get in touch.
What is the difference between a landing page and a website?+
The website informs everyone who drops by. The landing page is for people who clicked a specific ad, and has only one offer and one action. That is why it converts better on ad traffic, and why we rarely send ads to the homepage.
What is a good conversion rate for a landing page?+
It depends mostly on what you sell. On search campaigns in the accounts we run, it ranged from 1.3% (saunas) to 19.2% (move-out cleaning in Oslo) in July–Sept 2026. Compare with your own industry and starting point, not with a cross-industry average. All the numbers.
What does it cost to have you make a landing page?+
The landing page has no separate price. We make it as part of the ad partnership, where we take a share of what we earn you instead of a fixed monthly fee. We work with established businesses with at least NOK 30,000 in monthly ad budget. See the pricing page.
Where should the landing page live?+
On your own domain. One of our pages first sat on a subdomain of ours, and Google rated the landing page experience “below average” even though the page converted well. We moved it to the client’s domain on 6 September 2026.
Do you make landing pages for Meta ads too?+
Yes. The principles are the same, but traffic from Facebook and Instagram hasn’t searched for you, so the page has to do more of the explaining itself. It should use the same images and message as the ad, so the transition feels like one thing.
