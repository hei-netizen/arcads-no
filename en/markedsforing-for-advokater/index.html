Marketing for law firms

Marketing for law firms, one practice area at a time.

Someone searching “advokat arbeidsrett” (employment lawyer) has a completely different case than someone searching “advokat arv” (inheritance lawyer), and each should see their own ad and their own page. Here is the setup we would use, and why we start by shutting out the searches you don’t want to pay for.

Short answer: A law firm should have one campaign per practice area it wants cases in, such as inheritance, employment law or separation and divorce, and not bid on “advokat” (lawyer) alone. “Gratis” (free), “fri rettshjelp” (legal aid) and “jusstudent” (law student) go in as negative keywords if you don’t offer that. The landing page says what the first consultation costs, and the form asks for the case type, so you avoid inquiries your lawyers won’t take.

One campaign per practice area

With one campaign per practice area, you decide where the budget goes. If you want more employment cases and fewer divorces, we move the money there. The ad can have the practice area in the headline, and the page it leads to is only about that. A shared “advokat [by]” (lawyer [city]) campaign gives you neither.

The searches you don’t want to pay for

Lawyer searches come from two groups: people who have a case and can pay, and people looking for free help. Both search “advokat arbeidsrett”. You separate them in three places. The ad copy says what it costs. The landing page says what the first consultation involves. And negative keywords keep out searches that never become cases:

  • Free help: gratis, fri rettshjelp, rettshjelp, Juss-Buss, Jusshjelpa, jusstudent
  • Jobs and studies: jobb (job), lønn (salary), stilling (position), utdanning (education), student
  • Do-it-yourself: mal (template), eksempel (example), skjema (form)

If you offer legal aid, it gets its own campaign with its own page, so those cases don’t get mixed in with the rest.

Let the form do the sorting

A form with only name and phone gives you inquiries your lawyers spend time calling back without knowing whether they’ll take the case. We would ask for the case type, a short description and the name of the opposing party. The last one lets you run a conflict check before you call.

Once you know which inquiries became cases, that can be sent back to Google as a separate conversion. Then bidding learns the difference between an inquiry and an engagement.

What an inquiry is worth

A NOK 40,000 case with a 60% contribution margin gives NOK 24,000. If one in four qualified inquiries becomes a case, an inquiry is worth NOK 6,000. That’s where the ads break even. We set the target below that, and track whether the inquiries from each practice area actually turn into cases.

The first few weeks

  1. Tracking on the form and phone, with calls over 60 seconds as a separate conversion
  2. One campaign per practice area you want more of
  3. Price and terms for the first consultation on every page
  4. Negative keywords from the list above, before the first krone is spent

After that, we go through the search terms every week, and you get a report every Monday with cost, inquiries and which practice areas they came from.

What law firms ask us.

What can a lead cost for a law firm?+
We don’t have our own numbers from the legal industry, so we work from the case value. A NOK 40,000 case with NOK 24,000 in contribution margin, where one in four qualified inquiries becomes a case, makes an inquiry worth around NOK 6,000. The target is set well below that, and we measure on inquiries that became cases, not on everyone who filled out the form.
Should we show prices in the ad?+
If you can, yes. An hourly rate or a fixed price for the first consultation in the ad means people who are only looking for free help don’t click. Every click you avoid is money saved.
How do we filter out people looking for legal aid?+
Negative keywords (gratis, fri rettshjelp, rettshjelp, jusstudent, Juss-Buss, Jusshjelpa) and the price in the ad. If you offer legal aid, we run it as a separate campaign with its own page.
Facebook for law firms?+
Rarely as the main channel, because people search when the case comes up. Retargeting people who have visited a practice area page can work, with factual content.
Have you worked with law firms before?+
No. The closest is Generalfinans, where the customers are businesses and every inquiry has to be assessed before it turns into anything. The setup on this page is how we would do it for you.
