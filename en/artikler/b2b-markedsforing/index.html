B2B marketing: Google Ads in practice

This article is based on one B2B account we manage: Generalfinans, which leases machinery and vehicles to businesses. It’s one data set, not an industry average, so I’m clear about what the numbers can and can’t tell you.

By Victor Skretteberg, Arcads · Updated September 28, 2026

Short answer: If you sell something businesses search for, Google Search is the natural place to start your B2B marketing. In the leasing account we manage, a lead cost NOK 517 from search ads (NOK 406 including branded searches), and 15% of search clicks turned into a form. LinkedIn only fits once a new customer is worth so much that expensive clicks and long sales cycles add up.

That’s 32 leads. The numbers are directional, not conclusive. The leads are forms that Google has recorded. The client also logs them in their own CRM, and the CRM is what we use as the source of truth for volume and quality. The inquiries are typically about vans, tractors and machinery.

Branded searches are separated out because they flatter the average: 7 of 32 leads came from people searching for the company name, for a total of NOK 55. It’s the cost of the search ads, NOK 517, that tells you what new customers cost.

Why Google Search first

Someone searching “leasing traktor” (tractor leasing) or “leasing varebil” (van leasing) has a concrete need. The clicks are expensive (NOK 79), but a large share of the people who click submit a form. With leasing contracts worth several hundred thousand kroner, the account can bear a cost per lead of a few hundred kroner.

The ads and landing page should speak the language of business: price excl. VAT, what can be leased, how fast they get a reply and who will get in touch. On September 6 we switched to a new landing page with a shorter form. The effect isn’t reflected in the numbers above. In September we also started a Performance Max campaign alongside, which we don’t have numbers from yet.

LinkedIn and Meta

LinkedIn lets you target by job title, industry and company size, which Google and Meta don’t do as precisely. The clicks are more expensive in return, and the B2B sales cycle is often long. We don’t have any LinkedIn ad numbers of our own to show, so I recommend starting with Google Search if your customers search, and considering LinkedIn when a new customer is worth a lot.

Meta can be used to reach people again who have visited your service pages. CEOs and buyers use Facebook and Instagram like everyone else. Generalfinans only advertises on Google for now, so we don’t have B2B numbers here either.

The form should qualify

A form with just name and email produces inquiries the salesperson can’t prioritize without calling first. Ask for what lets you prioritize. The form on the leasing landing page asks what they want to lease and the approximate amount, the company name and organization number, in addition to contact details. Then the salesperson can call with an offer.

Measure what becomes sales

Measure what is one step from a sale: a submitted form with a need, a booked meeting, a quote request. Guide downloads and webinar sign-ups don’t pay any bills. The next step is to send back to Google which leads turned into deals (offline conversions), so bidding learns from sales and not just from forms.

The decision takes time

  • Reach visitors again over 30–90 days, with different messages over time: a reference, the price, “get in touch.”
  • Content that answers the questions in the process, for example “lease or buy.” It can be used both on the website and in ads.
  • One person who owns the follow-up. A B2B lead left unanswered for days often goes to whoever replied first.

The setup

  1. Tracking on forms, phone and booked meetings, with a value per type.
  2. Google Search on the commercial searches, with negative keywords for “jobb” (job), “lønn” (salary), “student” and “privat” (private).
  3. Branded searches in their own campaign, so you see what new customers cost.
  4. One landing page per service with price excl. VAT and a form that qualifies.
  5. Offline conversions from the CRM once you have enough deals for it to make sense.

Questions and answers.

Does Google Ads work for B2B?+
In the one B2B account we manage, business leasing, 15% of search clicks turned into a form, and a lead cost NOK 517 from search ads (NOK 406 including branded searches) in July–September 2026. That’s 32 leads, so the numbers are directional.
Should a small B2B business use LinkedIn ads?+
Only when a new customer is worth so much that expensive clicks and long sales cycles add up. LinkedIn can target by job title and industry, which Google and Meta don’t do as precisely. We don’t have any LinkedIn ad numbers of our own, so start with Google Search if your customers search.
What is a good B2B lead?+
One that tells you who they are (company name, organization number), what they need and roughly how big it is, so the salesperson can call with an offer. The form should qualify, not just collect as many inquiries as possible.
What does B2B marketing cost?+
You set the ad budget yourself. At NOK 517 per lead, and with smart bidding needing around 15–30 conversions a month, we’re talking about approx. NOK 8,000–15,500 a month for one campaign with target CPA. You can start lower with “Maximize conversions” without a target, but then learning is slower.
