How much does a lead cost in Norway?

Numbers from five Norwegian Google Ads accounts we manage, June 9 to September 6, 2026. With and without branded searches, Search and Performance Max separately, and with the caveats that belong to each number.

By Victor Skretteberg, Arcads · Updated September 28, 2026

Short answer: With branded searches left out, a lead from Google Ads costs between roughly NOK 190 and 520 for the service businesses we manage, and around NOK 1,090 for high-ticket saunas. Whether the price is good for you depends on what a new customer is worth and what share of your leads become customers.

The numbers

All numbers come from the Google Ads accounts of five of our clients, active campaigns, June 9 – September 6, 2026. For business leasing the period is July 3 – September 6. We use numbers from July 3, when tracking was set up the way we wanted it. “Lead” means whatever the account counts as a conversion, and that isn’t quite the same in all five. That is covered under caveats.

The cost per click for move-out cleaning is an average of two Search campaigns: NOK 57 in the main Oslo campaign and NOK 50 in the campaign for the surrounding municipalities. Branded searches cost NOK 4–13 per click in the same five accounts.

A PMax click is cheaper because PMax also buys placements on YouTube, Display and Gmail, where clicks are cheap and intent is lower than in a search. So never compare cost per click across the two. Compare cost per lead.

What the numbers show

Cost per click explains little. Saunas have the cheapest search click (NOK 14) and the most expensive lead. Business leasing has the most expensive click (NOK 79), but 15% of the people who click submit a form. What decides it is how many of the people who click get in touch. A sauna costing tens of thousands of kroner is a purchase people spend weeks on, and many click to check prices long before they are ready.

Region matters a lot. For facade and roof cleaning, cost per lead ranged from NOK 145 in Trondheim (85 leads) to NOK 341 in Østfold (9 leads, a new region with little data), for the same service, same client and same period. Oslo came in at NOK 237. With one national campaign, Google would have allocated the money itself, and you wouldn’t have seen the difference. How we build accounts with many regions.

Branded searches move the average. In the facade cleaning account, 95 of 549 leads came from branded searches, at NOK 47 each. Include them and the average drops from approx. NOK 188 to 163. In the leasing account, 7 of 32 leads came from branded searches for a total of NOK 55, which pulls the average from NOK 517 to 406. If you get a report with cost per lead, ask whether branded searches are included.

“Retargeting” in PMax looks cheapest, but it isn’t a clean finding. Both the move-out cleaning and moving company accounts have a PMax campaign aimed at previous visitors, and both have the account’s lowest cost per lead (NOK 116 and 310). Much of that is attribution. The campaign reaches people who have already been on the site, often after arriving through a search ad, and gets the credit when they come back and submit a form. Some of them would have gotten in touch anyway. You only see how much the campaign actually adds if you turn it off for a period or in an area and watch the total.

What a lead should cost you

The table tells you what a lead costs. Whether that is expensive or cheap depends on what a lead is worth to you:

Max cost per lead = gross profit per new customer × share of leads that become customers.
If a customer is worth NOK 8,000 in gross profit and one in four inquiries becomes a customer, you can afford NOK 2,000 per lead. If a customer is worth NOK 1,500 and one in ten becomes a customer, you can afford NOK 150.

This is the number we use when we set target CPA in accounts with smart bidding. If you set the target higher than the customer can bear, Google will happily buy more leads, but you lose money on every one of them.

Caveats

  • Facade and roof cleaning counts “every” conversion per click. If the same person submits a form twice after one click, it counts twice. That causes some double counting, so the real number of unique leads is slightly lower and the cost per lead slightly higher than the table shows. Phone calls aren’t counted in this account, so some leads are also lost the other way.
  • Move-out cleaning includes clicks on the phone number and email (65 of 244, or 27%). These are clicks, not calls that actually took place. Counting only submitted forms, the price is approx. NOK 276 per lead.
  • The moving company number is bookings via form, measured on the thank-you page. Signed contracts are not included.
  • The sauna number mixes several types: contact form, popup, chatbot, Calendly bookings and signed orders. Leads alone cost NOK 978 on average. The value of a lead varies a lot by type, so the account is managed on value and not on cost per lead.
  • Business leasing has only 32 leads. The number is directional, not conclusive.
  • A lead is not a customer. How many become customers depends a lot on how quickly you call back.
  • Season. June–August is low season for facade cleaning and high season for moving. The numbers fluctuate by month.
  • One account per industry. These are not industry averages, just what we measure in the accounts we manage. Your account may be above or below.

Levers that move cost per lead

  1. A dedicated landing page per service, with the form visible without scrolling on mobile. The share of clicks that become leads ranges from under 1% to 24% in the table, and the landing page is a big part of the explanation.
  2. A separate campaign per region, so you see the difference and can move budget to where leads are cheapest.
  3. Review search terms every week. Searches with “selv” (DIY), “jobb” (job), “lønn” (salary) and “gratis” (free) are mostly people who aren’t going to buy anything.
  4. Branded searches in their own campaign, so the new-customer numbers aren’t flattered.
  5. Correct conversion counting. For leads, Google should count one conversion per click, and only what you actually want to pay for should be a primary conversion.

Questions and answers.

What is a normal cost per lead in Norway?+
There is no single normal number. In the accounts we manage, a lead from Google Ads, excluding branded searches, cost approx. NOK 188 for facade and roof cleaning, NOK 202 for move-out cleaning in Oslo, NOK 412 for a moving booking in Oslo and NOK 517 for business leasing (June–September 2026). High-ticket saunas came in around NOK 1,090. Whether the price is good depends on what a new customer is worth to you.
How much does a click on Google Ads cost in Norway?+
In the five accounts in this article, a search click excluding branded searches cost NOK 14–79: NOK 14 for saunas, NOK 20 for facade and roof cleaning, NOK 50–55 for moving and move-out cleaning in Oslo and NOK 79 for business leasing. Clicks in Performance Max cost NOK 6–28, and branded searches NOK 4–13. More click prices, including for online stores.
What is a good conversion rate for leads?+
In Search campaigns excluding branded searches, 11–15% of clicks became leads for facade cleaning, moving and business leasing, and 24% for move-out cleaning, where clicks on phone and email are counted. Saunas were under 1%, because it is a big purchase people take a long time over. Compare with accounts that sell something similar, not with a general average.
Are branded searches included in the numbers?+
The table shows both. In the facade cleaning account, 95 of 549 leads came from people searching for the company name, at NOK 47 each. With them the average is NOK 163, without them approx. NOK 188. Always ask whether branded searches are included when someone reports cost per lead.
How do I work out what a lead can cost?+
Multiply the gross profit per new customer by the share of leads that become customers. If a customer is worth NOK 8,000 and one in four inquiries buys, you can afford NOK 2,000 per lead. Only scale campaigns that come in below that number.
