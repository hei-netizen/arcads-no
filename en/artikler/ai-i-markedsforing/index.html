What AI does for us, and what a human does

We use AI for the work that used to take the most time and the least judgement. The decisions that cost a client money, we make ourselves. This is how the work is split:

TaskWhat AI doesWhat we do
VoiceoverCreates a Norwegian voice from the script, in several versionsWrite the script, listen to every version and choose
Motion adsWrites the code that animates the scenesDecide angle and hook, approve every frame that goes out
Search termsGoes through the search terms report and suggests negative keywordsApprove or reject each suggestion before anything changes
Ad copyFirst drafts of headlines and descriptionsFact-check every claim against the client’s website, rewrite
ReportsPulls numbers from Google Ads and Meta and lays them outRead the numbers and decide what to adjust
Budgets, bids and pausingNothingEverything. No budget or status change without Victor saying yes

The last point is deliberate. We let AI suggest, but not press buttons that change something already running in a client’s account. A mistake there costs real money before anyone notices.

Ad production: where AI saves the most time

Motion ads with Norwegian voiceover are the clearest example. We make them without a designer, without filming and without After Effects, using six tools:

  • Claude Code writes the script, plans the scenes and codes the animation.
  • Remotion turns the code into video, both 9:16 and 1:1 from the same file.
  • ElevenLabs creates the voice, music at the exact length, and afterwards checks that the voice said what is in the script (speech to text).
  • ffmpeg adjusts the audio level and pulls stills for review.

What AI doesn’t do is decide what the ad should say. That is where results are decided. And several of the rules we follow exist because AI got it wrong the first time:

  • The older multilingual voice model drifted into Danish on Norwegian text. We now use eleven_v4 with language code “no”.
  • Lots of short sentences made the voice sound choppy. We now write few, long sentences.
  • We make 3 to 6 versions of the voice and check each with speech to text, but always listen ourselves before anything is published.
  • Drawn mockups were rejected. Real screenshots and product images as the base work better.

The full recipe, with the templates we use, is in how we make motion ads with AI. We also make statics and UGC in-house, but the UGC videos have real people in front of the camera. More on creative production.

Analysis: AI sorts, we decide

Every week a script pulls the search terms from the Google Ads accounts we manage. AI sorts them and suggests negative keywords, placements to exclude and ads that underperform. The suggestions come to us, not straight into the account. An example of what the review finds: in the move-out cleaning account, the search “billig flyttevask oslo” (cheap move-out cleaning Oslo) cost NOK 298 in June and July without a single lead. It is now a negative keyword.

The numbers in the tables on arcads.no, like the conversion rates in the glossary, are pulled the same way, straight from the Google Ads API, and read by a human before they are published.

Google’s own AI campaigns, and when they fool you

Much of “AI in marketing” already happens inside the ad account, whether you want it or not. Three things are now run by Google’s machine learning:

  • Performance Max chooses where ads appear: Search, Shopping, YouTube, Gmail and the Display network.
  • Broad match lets Google show the ad on searches it thinks mean the same as your keyword.
  • Smart bidding sets the bid per auction based on how likely Google thinks a conversion is.

All three can work well. A Performance Max campaign set up for retargeting delivered leads at NOK 116 for move-out cleaning in Oslo, against NOK 211 on search, and NOK 310 against NOK 467 for the moving company (9 June–6 Sept 2026). But the same campaign also showed ads in mobile games and in children’s videos on YouTube. Nobody there books move-out cleaning. That is why we check the placement report regularly and exclude what doesn’t belong.

Broad match has the same weakness: Google interprets the search, and the interpretation can be wrong. It is the search terms report that shows what you actually pay for, not the keyword list.

AI learns from what you count. Smart bidding is never better than the conversions it gets. When we started with Leid.no, tracking counted far more purchases than actually happened. An algorithm optimising toward the wrong numbers just gets faster at being wrong. That is why we set up tracking before spending a single krone on ads.

What we don’t use AI for

  • Fake customers. We don’t make AI images or AI video of “happy customers”. The people in our ads are real, and we never show a person who isn’t the one speaking.
  • Fake reviews and quotes. The quotes we use are real and named, like “We’re getting more leads than ever!! Amazing” from Elliot at Badstugutta.
  • Made-up numbers and promises. AI will happily write “10x more customers” if you let it. Every claim in an ad must be on the client’s own website or in our numbers, and we fact-check before anything is published.
  • Before and after images for clinics. Neither real nor generated.
  • Decisions about money. Budgets, bids, pausing and deleting we do ourselves, after reading the numbers.

The reason is simple: an ad that promises something the business can’t deliver brings expensive clicks and disappointed customers. And an AI-generated “customer” who gets exposed costs more trust than it ever won.

Should you use AI in your own marketing?

Yes, for drafts and groundwork, with one rule: nothing goes out until a human has read it, heard it or seen it. Start with whatever takes the most time for you today. For many, that is ad copy and images in several formats. Then go through the campaigns Google already runs with AI, and check placements and search terms.

Would you rather we did it? We take a share of what we earn you, not a fixed monthly fee. If you don’t earn more, you pay us nothing. For it to work for both sides, the ads need to generate enough for our share to be around NOK 20–30,000 a month. Questions and answers.

What can AI be used for in marketing?+
Drafts and groundwork: voiceover, animated ads, first drafts of ad copy, sorting search terms and laying out reports. Among other tools, we use Claude Code, Remotion and ElevenLabs for motion ads with a Norwegian voice. Decisions about budget and what the ads promise are made by a human.
Does Norwegian AI voiceover work in ads?+
With the right model and script, yes. We use ElevenLabs eleven_v4 with language code “no”, write few and long sentences, make 3 to 6 versions and always listen ourselves before publishing. The older multilingual model drifted into Danish on Norwegian text.
Are Performance Max and broad match AI?+
Yes, both are run by Google’s machine learning. They can deliver good results: a PMax campaign for retargeting gave leads at NOK 116 against NOK 211 on search. But it also showed ads in mobile games and children’s videos, so placements and search terms need regular checks.
Do you use AI-generated images of customers?+
No. The people in our ads are real, we never show a person who isn’t the one speaking, and we don’t use fake reviews or quotes. Claims in the ads are fact-checked against the client’s own website.
Can AI replace a marketing agency?+
It can do much of the groundwork, which is why we can be two people. But AI guesses, and it doesn’t know whether tracking counts correctly, whether a lead became a customer or whether a claim is true. Someone still has to read the numbers and take responsibility for the decisions.
