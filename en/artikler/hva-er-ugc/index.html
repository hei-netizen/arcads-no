What is UGC?

UGC stands for user-generated content. In ads, it means video and images that look like they were filmed by an ordinary person on their phone. Here’s what it is, why it works, what it doesn’t fix, and how to get it.

By Jona Helgen, Arcads · Updated September 28, 2026

Short answer: UGC (user-generated content) is video and images that look like they were filmed by an ordinary person on their phone, used in a company’s own ads. It works because it looks like the content people otherwise stop for in their feed, not like advertising. UGC isn’t influencer marketing: an influencer lends you their audience, while a UGC creator makes content you own and run in your own ads.

The definition

UGC stands for user-generated content. In practice, it’s video and images that look like they were filmed by an ordinary person on their phone. Someone unboxing a product on the kitchen counter. Someone talking straight into the selfie camera about something she’s tried. Natural light, slightly uneven sound, no studio.

Still, most of the UGC you see in ads isn’t made by random customers. It’s made by creators who get paid for it, or cut from raw footage the company has collected. “User-generated” describes how the content looks, not necessarily who made it.

Why UGC works in ads

On Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat, your ad competes with content from friends and people users follow. Anything that looks like a commercial gets scrolled past. A person talking straight to the camera looks like what people are there to see, and so gets a few more seconds of attention. Those seconds are what the ad has to say something.

What separates a good UGC ad from a bad one is rarely image quality. It’s the first two to three seconds (the hook), that it’s shot vertically (9:16), and that it has on-screen text for everyone watching without sound. Meta and TikTok also find the audience based on what’s in the ad, so a focused video performs better than a broad one.

UGC isn’t always the winner. For Mokki (sunglasses for kids), simple image ads also worked well. CMO Mattis Ohana Goksøyr wrote to us: “Wow, even the statics are working so well! Great job, guys, fantastic.” That’s why we always test several formats against each other.

When we ran the ads for Leid.no (trailer rental), we made UGC, image ads and Snapchat formats side by side and let the numbers decide. The account ended up at 4.73x ROAS across channels, on more than NOK 100,000 a month in ads. It wasn’t one video that did it, but enough variants to find the ones that worked.

Common types of UGC ads

  • Review: a person tells you what she bought, why, and what happened next.
  • Demo or unboxing: the product shown in use, ideally with hands in the shot.
  • Problem and solution: opens with a problem the customer recognizes, then shows the solution.
  • Founder or employee on camera: works well for service businesses, where people buy from someone they trust.

UGC isn’t influencer marketing

An influencer lends you their audience. You pay for their reach, and the content lives on their channel. A UGC creator makes content you use in your own ads, aimed at your own audience, and doesn’t need a single follower. You’re buying the content, not the reach.

The two can be combined. With partnership ads, you run the ad from the creator’s profile, but with your budget and your targeting. For Mokki, we recruited creators with detailed scripts and ran the ads that way.

How to get UGC

  • Ask your customers to film. Free and authentic, but it’s slow, the quality is uneven, and you can’t control what they say. Good as a supplement.
  • Hire a UGC creator. Faster and easier to direct. Expect a few thousand kroner per video in Norway. (Are you a creator yourself? We’re recruiting.) The most common mistake is buying one video and running it for months. Ads wear out, so you need new variants regularly.
  • Have it made continuously as part of running your ads. That’s how we work: we write hooks, edit raw footage, make 10+ new ads a week and scale the ones that work. We’ve made more than 5,000 ads for our clients. Our UGC service and our full production.

What UGC doesn’t fix

If your tracking is broken or your landing page makes it hard to find the price or the form, it doesn’t matter how authentic the video feels. Fix the measurement and the page first, then the content. Clinics and aesthetic treatments have their own rules: no before and after images, not even in UGC.

Questions and answers.

What does UGC mean?+
UGC stands for user-generated content. In ads, it means video and images that look like they were made by an ordinary person on their phone, not by an agency with a studio. It can be made by real customers, by paid creators, or cut from raw footage. “User-generated” describes how it looks, not necessarily who made it.
Why does UGC work in ads?+
Because it looks like the content people otherwise see in their feed, not like advertising. A person talking straight to the camera gets a few more seconds of attention than a commercial. What decides it is the hook in the first seconds, a vertical format, and on-screen text for people watching without sound.
Is UGC the same as influencer marketing?+
No. An influencer lends you their audience, and you pay for the reach. UGC is the content itself: you use it in your own ads, aimed at your own audience, and the creator doesn’t need followers. The two can be combined with partnership ads.
How do I get UGC for my ads?+
You can ask your customers to film (authentic, but slow and uneven), hire UGC creators (faster, typically a few thousand kroner per video), or have it made continuously as part of running your ads. Whichever route you take, you need new variants regularly, because ads wear out.
