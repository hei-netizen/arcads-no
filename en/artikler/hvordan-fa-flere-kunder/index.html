How to get more customers: find out where you lose them

Most established businesses lose customers somewhere between the search and the signed deal. Check the five places it leaks, fix the biggest hole first, and then work out what more customers will cost you.

By Victor Skretteberg, Arcads · Published October 2, 20269 min read

Short answer: You get more customers by finding out where you lose them: are you found when people look, do they click on you, do they get in touch, do you reply fast enough, and do they come back? In the search campaigns we run for local services, 8–19% of the people who clicked got in touch (July–September 2026). If your page is below that, it is the cheapest place to start. If you are above it, you need more visitors, and Google search for “service + city” is the fastest way to get them.

Five places customers disappear

When a business that already advertises says it needs more customers, the answer is rarely “more visibility”. More often people drop off in one specific place: they don’t find you, they pick someone else in the search results, they leave the page, they wait too long for a reply, or they forget you. Each place has its own fix, and the fixes cost very different amounts. That is why it pays to find the leak before you spend more money.

1. Are you found when people look?

Start by checking that the demand exists. Keyword Planner in Google Ads is free and shows roughly how many people search for what you sell. A few examples from Norway (Google Keyword Planner, Oct 2026): “rørlegger oslo” (plumber Oslo) gets 2,400 searches a month, “rørlegger bergen” 1,300 and “rørlegger trondheim” 1,000. “Flyttevask oslo” (end-of-tenancy cleaning Oslo) gets 1,000. These are people with a need right now.

Then run the search yourself, on your phone, where you operate. If you are not on the first screen, those searches go to someone else. Organic rankings take months to build. A search campaign on “service + city”, limited to the area you actually cover, can be live in a few days. If search volume is close to zero, demand has to be created, and Facebook and Instagram are a better place to start.

From the accounts: Utvendig Renhold (roof and facade cleaning) has one search campaign per region, nine in total. Over 90 days (June–September 2026) that produced 549 leads. Trondheim alone delivered 85 leads at NOK 145 each.

2. Do they click on you?

Being there is not enough. Your ad sits next to three others and a map. What makes people pick you is seeing their service and area in the headline, a price or a starting price, and something concrete such as “available next week” or “fixed price before we start”.

The other half is avoiding the wrong clicks. The search terms report shows what people actually typed before they clicked. Read it every week and add negative keywords for anything that never turns into customers.

From the accounts: In the end-of-tenancy cleaning account we run in Oslo, the search “billig flyttevask oslo” (cheap end-of-tenancy cleaning Oslo) cost NOK 298 without producing a single inquiry (June 9–Sept 6, 2026). It became a negative keyword. At the moving company with the same owner, people searching for the company name cost around NOK 44 per click in a regular search campaign. They got their own brand campaign with a NOK 15 cap.

3. Do they get in touch?

Conversion rate is the share of clicks that become an inquiry. It is the most important number on this list, because it decides what each inquiry costs. Double the rate from 5 to 10% and you get twice as many inquiries for the same budget.

Two caveats. Searches for the company name are included in several of the accounts, and they convert better than other searches. And an expensive product like a sauna converts very differently from a cleaning job, so compare yourself with something similar.

Also look at mobile and desktop separately. In the end-of-tenancy cleaning account, 20.2% got in touch on desktop and 10.7% on mobile. For business leasing it was 18.8% against 7.8%. The exception was roof and facade cleaning, where 4,258 of 5,490 clicks came from mobile and mobile converted best (11.0% against 10.0%). If your mobile number is far below desktop, the form or the phone number on mobile is often the problem.

The biggest fix is sending people to a page about exactly what they searched for, not to the home page.

From the accounts: The new landing page we built for a leasing client (B2B) delivered a 16.1% conversion rate and NOK 412 per inquiry in its first 30 days (as of Aug 13, 2026). For Utvendig Renhold we built 18 local pages, one per branch, and switched 102 ads over to them on Aug 28, 2026.

4. Do you reply fast enough?

This is the leak that doesn’t show in the ad account. There, the inquiry looks like a success, but whether it becomes a customer is decided by what happens in the next few hours. Many customers send the same request to several businesses and go with whoever calls first.

Test it yourself: send a request through your own form on a Tuesday afternoon and note how long it takes before someone gets in touch. Then check what happens to an inquiry that arrives on a Friday evening. The fixes are simple: one named person owns the inbox and the phone, the customer gets an automatic confirmation that the inquiry was received, and if you have several branches, the inquiry goes straight to the right one based on the postcode.

From the accounts: Utvendig Renhold received 142 leads in a single week. That is around 20 a day across nine regions, and each one has to reach the right branch the same day for the ad money to turn into jobs.

5. Do they come back?

Most people who visit your page don’t get in touch the first time. They compare, ask their partner or get interrupted. Retargeting shows ads to people who have visited, in Google and on Facebook and Instagram, while they are still deciding. These are almost always the cheapest inquiries in an account, because people already know you.

If you have past customers, they are part of this too. An email or text message when it is natural to buy again costs almost nothing.

From the accounts: At the moving company, an inquiry from retargeting cost NOK 310, against an average of NOK 413 for the whole account (June 9–Sept 6, 2026).

Which hole should you fix first?

Take them in order of cost. Reply time is free to fix. A better landing page is a one-off job that makes every krone of ad spend worth more. More traffic is the only thing that costs money every month, so you do it last, once everything else holds water.

Rule of thumb: if your conversion rate from search is far below what we saw for local services (8–19%), start with the page. If you are at that level and still get too few inquiries, it is volume you are missing.

The three fastest ways to more customers this month

  1. Google search for what people are already looking for. People who type “flyttevask oslo” have made up their mind. The search campaign for end-of-tenancy cleaning in Oslo had a 26.9% conversion rate and NOK 211 per inquiry (June 9–Sept 6, 2026). How to get started: advertising on Google.
  2. Retargeting people who have already visited. For the end-of-tenancy cleaning client, an inquiry from retargeting cost NOK 116, against NOK 202 for the account as a whole. All it takes is working tracking and a page that has had visitors.
  3. An offer on the landing page. Give people a reason to get in touch now: a fixed price, available dates, or a sale for an online store. QualiPet got 10.3x ROAS on Meta during a sales campaign (Sept 2026).

Work out what more customers cost

Work backwards from the number of customers, not forwards from a budget. You need four numbers: how many new customers you want, what share of inquiries become customers, what share of clicks become inquiries, and what a click costs. The buttons fill in conversion rate and cost per click from the search campaigns we run (July–September 2026). Only you know the share that becomes customers, so use your own number.

An example with the numbers from roof and facade cleaning: if you want 10 new customers and every third inquiry becomes a customer, you need 30 inquiries. At a 10.8% conversion rate that is around 280 clicks, and at NOK 18 per click around NOK 5,000 a month. The same sum with the moving company’s numbers (8.5% and NOK 47) comes to around NOK 16,600. The difference comes almost entirely from the cost per click and the page.

Google needs around 15–30 conversions in 30 days in a campaign for smart bidding to work well. If the sum gives you fewer than that, you can still advertise, but the campaign should be narrow and managed more closely. Do it yourself or get help?

You can do everything above yourself. Checking reply time and search volume takes an afternoon. What takes time is the weekly management: reading search terms, adding negative keywords, moving budget between areas and making new ads. If you do it yourself, set aside 2–3 hours a week.

Questions and answers.

How do I get more customers quickly?+
The fastest route is Google search for what people are already looking for, such as “service + city”, sent to a page about exactly that service. A campaign like that can be live in a few days. If your page already has visitors, retargeting the people who visited is the second fastest. For end-of-tenancy cleaning in Oslo, an inquiry from retargeting cost NOK 116, against an average of NOK 202 for the account (June–September 2026).
How do I get more customers without raising the budget?+
Increase the share who get in touch and the share who become customers. Send ads to one page per service instead of the home page, make the form and phone number easy on mobile, reply to inquiries the same day, and add negative keywords for searches that never produce customers. Double the conversion rate and you get twice as many inquiries for the same budget.
How much do I need to spend on ads to get more customers?+
Work backwards: the number of customers divided by the share of inquiries that become customers gives the number of inquiries. Divide by the conversion rate to get clicks, and multiply by the cost per click to get the budget. With numbers from roof and facade cleaning (10.8% and NOK 18) and every third inquiry becoming a customer, 10 new customers cost around NOK 5,000 a month.
Why do I get visitors but no inquiries?+
Usually because people land on a page that isn’t about what they searched for, because the form is clumsy on mobile, or because the traffic comes from the wrong searches. Check conversion rate on mobile and desktop separately and read the search terms report. In the search campaigns we run for local services, 8–19% got in touch (July–September 2026).
Should I use an agency to get more customers?+
You can do the check in this article yourself. Ongoing management takes 2–3 hours a week. We take a share of what we earn you, not a fixed monthly fee, and work with established businesses with a monthly ad budget of at least NOK 30,000.
