Advertising for clinics: dentists, physiotherapy and aesthetics.

We run Meta ads and landing pages for Frøyd, an aesthetic clinic in Norway, and Meta for a dental clinic in England. Here is how we set up ads for a clinic, what is different for dentists and physiotherapists, and what you are not allowed to say.

Short answer: Google Ads suits treatments people search for themselves, like “tannlege akutt” (emergency dentist), “implantat pris” (implant price) or “fysioterapeut uten henvisning” (physiotherapist without referral). Meta suits treatments people need to be made aware of, like skin boosters and microneedling. How we advertise for clinics.

Clinics have high-value treatments with low search volume, and rules about what they can promise. That’s why the choice of channel and the landing page matter more than the budget.

Channel

Google for what people search for, Meta for what they didn’t know about

“Tannlege Skien” (dentist Skien) and “tannregulering pris” (orthodontics price) are searched every day, and Google handles that. Skin boosters, PDRN and microneedling have to be shown to the right people before they search. Meta does that, with video of the practitioner and the treatment.

Landing page

One page per treatment

Don’t send the patient to the homepage. One page per treatment with what it is, what it costs or what the booking fee is, who performs it, and a booking form. For Frøyd, microneedling and PDRN skin booster each have their own page.

Tracking

Bookings, not clicks

We set up a conversion on completed bookings and count forms and phone calls separately. That way you know what a new patient costs, and Google and Meta optimize toward real bookings.

Content

The practitioner on camera

Clinics win on trust. Short videos where the practitioner explains the treatment work better than a polished commercial. We write the script, edit, and make new variations every week.

Regulations

What you can say, and what you can’t

Advertising for health services must be responsible, sober and factual (Section 13 of the Norwegian Health Personnel Act). No promised results, no before/after images, the correct title for the practitioner, and “free” only when it is free.

Offers

Promotions that fill open slots

A time-limited offer on one treatment, with the regular price and the promotional price side by side. It fills the calendar without you having to lower prices across your whole treatment menu.

Advertising for dentists

The searches we would build the account around: “tannlege [by]” (dentist [city]), “akutt tannlege” (emergency dentist), “tannbleking pris” (teeth whitening price), “implantat pris” (implant price), “tannregulering voksne” (adult orthodontics). “Tannlege [by]” is the most general search, and often where competition is fiercest. The treatment searches are more specific and tell you what the patient needs, so they deserve their own ad groups with their own page.

A page that says “Implant from NOK 18,900, performed by [name], 15 minutes from the city center, book here” answers what the patient is wondering: what it costs, who does it and where you are. A general clinic page answers none of that. The price is just an example, use your own.

Work backwards before you spend a single krone

An implant at NOK 25,000 with a NOK 10,000 contribution margin, where one in three inquiries becomes a booking, makes an inquiry worth around NOK 3,300. In theory, that’s how much you can pay and break even. We set the target well below that, and bid higher on implants and orthodontics than on “tannlege [by]”.

What we would fix first

  • The whole budget on “tannlege [by]”. It’s expensive and says little about what the patient needs.
  • No prices on the website. Patients who can’t find a price move on to a clinic that shows it.
  • “Free consultation” in the ad and a booking fee on the page.
  • Booking only by phone during opening hours.
  • “Covered by Helfo” in the ad. The subsidy only applies to specific conditions, and the dentist has to assess that.

Advertising for physiotherapists and chiropractors

The patient is in pain right now and searches “fysioterapeut [by]” (physiotherapist [city]), “kiropraktor nær meg” (chiropractor near me) or “fysioterapi uten henvisning” (physiotherapy without referral). Many don’t know they can go straight to a physiotherapist without seeing a doctor first. If the ad and the page say “no referral needed”, you answer the question before they get to ask it.

The other thing the patient wonders about is price, and here the municipal operating grant decides it. With an operating agreement with the municipality, the patient pays a co-payment. Without one, they pay full price. State both clearly, or the patient gets unsure and doesn’t book.

The complaints are searches of their own: “vondt i ryggen behandling” (back pain treatment), “nakkesmerter” (neck pain), “naprapat [by]” (naprapath [city]). We would put them in their own ad groups with a page that answers exactly that complaint, so you can see what they cost compared with searches for the profession.

The math

A course of five sessions at NOK 650 each brings in NOK 3,250 in revenue. With a 60% contribution margin and every other booking becoming a patient, an inquiry is worth around NOK 975. That’s much less than for a dental clinic, so the account has to be tighter: a short radius around the clinic, ads when you can take bookings, and online booking that shows open slots.

What we would fix first

  • Booking that requires the patient to call during opening hours.
  • No price, or no explanation of the co-payment and operating grant.
  • Ads only on “fysioterapeut” (physiotherapist), none on the complaints.
  • A large radius. People rarely travel far for a treatment they’ll go to several times.

After launch we go through the search terms every week, move budget to where bookings are cheapest, and you get a report every Monday with what you spent and what you got.

What clinics ask us.

Google Ads or Meta for a clinic?+
Google for treatments people search for when they need them: dentist, emergency dentist, implants, physiotherapist, chiropractor. Meta for treatments people don’t look for until they’ve seen them: skin boosters, microneedling, whitening. Start with the channel where demand already exists, and add the other once tracking is in place.
What does a new patient from ads cost?+
It depends on the treatment, so we work backwards before we start. An implant at NOK 25,000 with a NOK 10,000 contribution margin, where one in three inquiries books, makes an inquiry worth around NOK 3,300. A course of physiotherapy is worth much less, so the cost per booking has to come down accordingly. That number decides how much we bid.
Can we use before/after images in the ads?+
We don’t. Under Section 13 of the Norwegian Health Personnel Act, advertising for health services must be responsible, sober and factual, and the Marketing Control Act applies as well. Before/after images of patients suggest a result no one can promise, so we don’t use them, not even for aesthetic treatments. Show the practitioner instead, what the treatment involves and what it costs.
Can we say “free consultation”?+
Only if it actually is free, with no booking fee and no requirement to buy a treatment afterwards. Otherwise it’s misleading. We check every ad against the clinic’s actual prices before it goes live.
What about Helfo, can we advertise prices?+
Yes, and you should show a price or a “from” price on every treatment page. Helfo only subsidizes dental treatment for adults for specific conditions that the dentist has to assess. So never write “covered by Helfo” in an ad. State the price without the subsidy, and say on the page that some treatments may qualify for a subsidy and that you check this at the first appointment.
Does the patient need a referral to see a physiotherapist?+
No, patients can go directly to a physiotherapist without a referral from a doctor. What the appointment costs depends on whether the clinic has an operating grant from the municipality. With an operating grant the patient pays a co-payment, without one they pay full price. State both clearly on the page, because that’s what the patient wonders about before booking.
Who should be in the videos?+
The practitioner. Patients choose a clinic based on trust, and a dentist or skin therapist explaining the treatment in 30 seconds builds more trust than a polished clinic film. We write the script and edit, you film on your phone.
How do you measure bookings?+
A conversion on the thank-you page after booking or in the booking system, with forms and phone calls as separate conversions. If the clinic offers several treatments, the form passes along which treatment the inquiry is about, so we can see what a booking costs per treatment.
What does Arcads cost?+
We take a share of the value we create for you, not a fixed monthly fee. If you don’t earn more, you pay us nothing. For this to work for both sides, the ads need to create enough value for our share to come to around NOK 20,000–30,000 a month.
